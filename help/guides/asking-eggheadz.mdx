Eggheadz is the team you ask. Open the panel, ask a question in your own words, and the answer arrives beside whatever you were already looking at.

## It opens beside your work, not over it

On a laptop or a desktop the panel sits against the right edge of the screen. Your page does not get any narrower when it opens, and it does not shuffle around when it closes.

Everything behind the panel keeps working while it is open:

- **Click anything on the page.** Buttons, rows and links all still work.
- **Scroll the page** while the answer is on screen.
- **Type into the page.** Editing an amount with eggheadz open is fine.
- **The page stays lit.** Nothing behind the panel is dimmed or greyed out.

So you can read a bill and ask about it at the same time, which is the point.

## Open it and close it

| What you want | How |
|---------------|-----|
| **Open the panel** | Click **Ask eggheadz** in the top bar |
| **Ask from anywhere, without the mouse** | ⌘J on a Mac, Ctrl+J on Windows |
| **Close it** | Click the **x** in the top right of the panel, or press **Esc** while your cursor is in it |

<Note>
⌘J opens a box you type straight into. Ask your question there and eggheadz answers in the panel. Press it again to put the box away.
</Note>

Pressing **Esc** only closes the panel when you are working inside it. If you are typing in the page behind, Esc belongs to the page and the panel stays put.

## Make it wider or narrower

Drag the left edge of the panel. Hover over it and the edge thickens and turns gold, so you can see what you are about to grab.

- **Anywhere from 320 to 640 pixels wide.** Past either end it slows down and springs back, so you can feel the limit.
- **It settles on three comfortable widths** as you pass them: 360, 400 and 480.
- **Double-click the edge** to put it back to 400.
- **Or use the keyboard.** Tab to the edge, then press the left and right arrow keys. Home makes it as narrow as it goes, End as wide.

## It remembers your width

The width you choose comes back next time, and so does whether you had the panel open.

It is remembered for **you**, not for the computer. Two people signing in on the same machine each get their own panel back.

<Note>
If your browser is in private mode, or has site storage turned off, the panel opens at 400 pixels every time. Everything else works normally.
</Note>

## Following a link keeps the conversation

Ask eggheadz what needs you, tap a row in the answer, and the record opens on the page beside the panel. The conversation stays where it is, ready for your next question.

## On a phone or a narrow window

Below about 1024 pixels wide there is no room to read a page and a conversation side by side. So the panel slides in from the right and covers the page instead, the way it always has. Close it to get back to what you were doing.

Tapping a row that opens a record closes the panel first, so it is not sitting on top of the record it found for you.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does the panel push my page over?">
    No. Your page is exactly the same width with the panel open as it is with the panel closed. The panel floats above the right-hand side of the screen rather than taking a slice of the page.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Will clicking the page by accident close the panel?">
    No. Clicking on the page behind does nothing to the panel. It closes when you close it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why does the top bar stay visible?">
    The panel starts below the top bar on purpose, so your navigation, search and account menu are all still one click away.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I use the eggz Chrome extension. Why does that panel narrow my page?">
    That one is Chrome's own side panel, and Chrome decides how it sits. The browser opens it in a strip beside the tab, so the page underneath makes room for it. The panel inside eggz is ours, which is why it can float over the page instead.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I have reduced motion turned on. What changes?">
    The panel fades in where it is instead of sliding across. Everything else behaves the same way.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
