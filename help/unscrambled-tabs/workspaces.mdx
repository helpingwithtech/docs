## What a workspace is

A **workspace** is a **saved set of browser tabs** with a name, a colour and an icon — one per client, project, or hat you wear. Switch to a workspace and your window becomes that project's tabs; switch away and everything is saved exactly as you left it, ready for next time.

## Create a workspace

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click New">
    In the **card or side panel**, click **New** beside the *Workspaces* heading. On the **dashboard**, click **New Workspace** at the foot of the open sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it and make it yours">
    Give it a name (up to 40 characters — "Birdsong Pottery", "Tax time", "Weekend reno"), then pick an **icon** and a **colour**. A live preview shows how the row will look.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Create Workspace">
    Done — and here's the nice part: **the tabs you have open right now are added automatically**, so your current session is instantly safe. (It captures the window you created it from.)
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
Other ways to make one: **Save all** on a window in the [Open Tabs view](/help/unscrambled-tabs/open-tabs#whole-window-moves) turns a whole window into a workspace; restoring a [session snapshot](/help/unscrambled-tabs/session-time-travel) creates one; and on the dashboard, **⌘K → Create workspace** does it from the keyboard.
</Tip>

## Switching — everything comes back {#switching}

Switching is the heart of the product, so here's exactly what happens. When you switch this window from workspace A to workspace B:

1. **A is saved first** — its tabs, their order, which tabs were pinned, your Chrome **tab groups** (names, colours, and whether they were collapsed), **which tab you were focused on**, and any [custom tab names](/help/unscrambled-tabs/manage-tabs#rename-any-tab).
2. **B opens in the same window** — and not just its tabs: its **tab groups are rebuilt**, **pinned tabs are pinned again**, your **custom tab names are back on the strip**, and the **exact tab you were on last time is focused**.

No mystery windows, no "roughly what you had". You leave a workspace mid-thought and come back to the same thought.

**Where to click:**

- **Card or side panel** — click the workspace's name (or the ▶ **Switch to workspace** button that appears on hover).
- **Dashboard sidebar** — clicking a row *shows* that workspace's tabs and links so you can peek without committing; to actually switch, use the **Switch** pill that appears when you hover the row, or the **Switch to this workspace** button in the header.
- **Keyboard** — the **Jump to a workspace** shortcut (**⌥A** on a Mac, **Alt+A** on Windows and Linux) opens the quick switcher: most-recent first, and **Enter flips you straight back to your previous workspace**. See [Keyboard shortcuts](/help/unscrambled-tabs/keyboard-shortcuts).
- **On the dashboard, ⌘K** (**Ctrl+K**) → **Switch workspace…** → pick one.

### Next / previous workspace

Two more commands — **Switch to the next workspace** and **Switch to the previous workspace** — step through your workspace list in order, wrapping around at the end. They ship without keys; give them keys at `chrome://extensions/shortcuts` and you can cycle workspaces without opening anything.

## One workspace per window

Each Chrome window remembers **its own** workspace. Switching only ever affects the window you're in, so you can keep one client on your left monitor and another on your right, each window showing its own world.

Want that second window without leaving your first? Hover a workspace row on the dashboard and click **Open in new window** — the workspace opens in a fresh Chrome window (groups, pins and all) and that window is now bound to it. Your current window doesn't change. The same button lives in the pinned-workspace flyouts on the slim sidebar.

## The row menu — two ways to open it {#row-menu}

Every workspace row has a **⋯** button that appears when you hover it. **Right-clicking the row does exactly the same thing** — same menu, same items, opened right where your cursor is. Use whichever is quicker.

<Note>
Right-click gives you the browser's own menu instead in two cases: while you're typing a new name into a row, and while you're ticking rows with checkboxes. In both, the row has no menu to offer.
</Note>

What's in it:

| Action | Card / side panel | Dashboard | What it does |
|--------|:---:|:---:|--------------|
| **Rename** | ✓ | ✓ | Type the new name (in place in the panel; a small dialog on the dashboard) |
| **Duplicate** | — | ✓ | Makes "*name* (copy)" right below the original — tabs, tab groups and saved links all copied. Great for a template you clone per client |
| **Change colour** | — | ✓ | Pick from the nine workspace colours |
| **Move to folder** | — | ✓ | File the workspace into any folder, or back out to no folder |
| **Pin to sidebar** / **Unpin from sidebar** | ✓ | ✓ | Pinned workspaces get their own **Pinned** section — and their own icons on the dashboard's slim sidebar, one click away all day |
| **Archive** | ✓ | ✓ | Files it away without deleting anything — see below |
| **Set emoji icon** / **Change emoji icon** | — | ✓ | Opens a searchable emoji picker; the emoji replaces the row's icon everywhere. **Reset to favicon** puts it back |
| **Delete** | ✓ | ✓ | Asks first — "This cannot be undone" means it |

<Note>
When a workspace has no emoji set, its row shows the icon of its first saved tab — so workspaces tend to look like what they contain, automatically.
</Note>

## Archiving — filing without deleting {#archiving}

Finished with a project but not ready to throw it away? **Archive** it.

An archived workspace:

- **Leaves every list** — Active, Pinned, its folder — and gathers in an **Archived** section at the very bottom, collapsed until you click it open. The heading shows the count, like **Archived (3)**.
- **Keeps everything** — its tabs, tab groups, custom tab names and saved links are all intact.
- **Is unpinned** if it was pinned, so it's out of the slim sidebar too.
- **Can't be switched into or dropped onto** — it's out of the way on purpose. Its row menu is reduced to just **Restore** and **Delete**, and hovering it offers **Restore** where other rows offer Switch.
- **Stays out of search results** and out of **Select all**, so a bulk action can't sweep up something you deliberately shelved.

**Restore** puts it straight back into the normal lists, exactly as it was (though not re-pinned — pin it again if you want it in the slim sidebar).

<Tip>
On the dashboard you can also press **⌘K** (**Ctrl+K**) and type *archive* → **Archive workspace…** → pick which one.
</Tip>

## Folders

Once you're past a handful of workspaces, group them into **folders** ("Clients", "Personal", "Archive"…):

- **Create** — hover the *Workspaces* heading in the card or side panel and click the folder icon beside **New**; on the dashboard, click the **+** next to **Folders**. Type the name, press Enter. (Or **⌘K → New folder**.)
- **Rename / Set emoji icon / Delete folder** — in the folder's **⋯** menu. Deleting a folder is gentle: **the workspaces inside are moved out, not deleted**.
- **Reorder** — drag a folder up or down in the dashboard's open sidebar; a line shows where it'll land.
- **Fill them** — on the dashboard, drag a workspace onto a folder (or onto its icon on the slim sidebar), use **⋯ → Move to folder**, or select several and move them together (below).

Folders remember whether you left them open or closed. And while you're dragging a tab, holding still over a **closed** folder for a moment springs it open so you can drop on a workspace inside — see [Moving tabs](/help/unscrambled-tabs/moving-tabs).

## Select several at once (bulk actions) {#select-several-at-once-bulk-actions}

On the dashboard's open sidebar you can act on many workspaces in one go:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start selecting">
    Hover any workspace or folder row — a faint checkbox appears on its left. Click it and you're in selection mode. Ticking a folder ticks everything inside it. **Select all** / **Deselect all** sits at the top of the list. (Archived workspaces are never included in **Select all**.)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Act on the selection">
    A bar appears at the bottom of the sidebar showing **"N selected"** with two buttons: **Move** (pick a folder, or no folder at all) and **Delete**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Changed your mind?">
    **Esc** clears the selection. And after a bulk delete, a message appears with an **Undo** button — click it within **5 seconds** and everything comes back exactly as it was.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Bulk-deleting a **folder** deletes the workspaces inside it too (unlike deleting a folder from its ⋯ menu, which moves them out first). The 5-second **Undo** is your safety net — after that, it's permanent.
</Warning>

## The tabs inside a workspace {#the-tabs-inside-a-workspace}

Select a workspace on the dashboard and its middle **Tabs** pane lists every saved tab. From there:

- **Click a tab** — if this workspace is the one your window is using, you jump to the live tab; otherwise the page opens in a new tab.
- **Hover for actions** — **Rename tab** (your name shows everywhere, see [renaming](/help/unscrambled-tabs/manage-tabs#rename-any-tab)), **Open in new tab**, and **Remove tab**.
- **Drag to reorder** the saved list, or **drag a tab onto another workspace** in the sidebar to move it there — see [Moving tabs](/help/unscrambled-tabs/moving-tabs).

<Warning>
**Remove tab** removes it from the workspace — and if that workspace is the active one, it also **closes the live browser tab**. There's no confirmation, so aim carefully.
</Warning>

Tabs are also saved automatically as you browse: with **Auto-save tabs** on (the default), the active workspace quietly keeps up with what you open and close, so switching away never loses anything.

## Resources — saved links {#resources-saved-links}

Every workspace also has **Resources**: links you want to *keep* without keeping a tab open. They live in the right-hand pane on the dashboard.

- **Add** — click **Add** in the Resources pane header, paste the address, optionally give it a title, **Save**.
- **From a tab** — click **Save as resource** on any row of the [Open Tabs view](/help/unscrambled-tabs/open-tabs#what-you-can-do-with-a-tab), drag a tab onto the Resources pane, or right-click any page → **Unscrambled Tabs** → **Save for later & close tab**.
- **Edit / Remove** — hover a resource for the pencil and ✕.

The magic is in the click: **click a resource and it opens in a new tab *and* graduates into the workspace's tabs** — it stops being a bookmark and starts being part of the session. (Want to peek without that? Hover and use **Open in new tab** instead, and the resource stays put.)

Resources can be grouped into named **sections**, and you can drag a tab straight onto a section heading to file it there.

In the card and side panel, the active workspace's resources appear as a **Resources** section — click one there and it simply opens in a new tab.

## Pinned tabs

Tabs you pin in Chrome are first-class citizens: pinning survives every switch and restart, and pinned tabs are **never** suspended by [Performance modes](/help/unscrambled-tabs/performance-and-ram) or closed by [Close duplicates](/help/unscrambled-tabs/manage-tabs#close-duplicate-tabs-in-one-click).

## Opening a page you already have open?

Unscrambled Tabs spots it and offers to jump you to the existing tab instead — see [the duplicate catcher](/help/unscrambled-tabs/manage-tabs#already-open-the-duplicate-catcher).
